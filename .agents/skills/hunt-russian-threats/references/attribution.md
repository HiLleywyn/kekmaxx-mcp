# Attribution discipline

## Classify the relationship

Use the narrowest supported statement:
1. **Artifact association:** the same hash, string, script or configuration occurs.
2. **Infrastructure association:** an observed host, certificate, zone or service served it during a stated interval.
3. **Operational control:** independent evidence connects a controlling account, access event, deployment or configuration to the operation.
4. **Actor attribution:** distinctive technical and operational evidence supports a named activity cluster, or a cited authoritative source attributes the specific operation.
5. **State sponsorship/tasking:** separate evidence supports government direction, employment, funding or commissioned activity.

Do not promote a relationship simply because several weak signals co-occur. Treat a copied IOC list as one source. Treat an unvalidated provider-side account number as a lookup handle until its semantics are confirmed.

## Weight evidence

Give substantial weight, where authentic and relevant, to:
- specific official findings, technical reports with independently reproducible evidence, and signed court/agency records;
- rare implant configuration/protocol/code characteristics plus compatible timing and independently established operational links;
- properly scoped, preserved account/deployment/audit records;
- a current bridge connecting a previously attributed campaign to new observations.

Use common IPs/ASNs, Russian registration or language, time zones, generic tools, template reuse, certificate issuers, political narratives, and commodity malware as contextual leads. None individually identifies nationality or state sponsorship. A malware-family match can support observed behavior without identifying the operator. A victim or reseller's infrastructure can carry an actor's artifacts.

A downstream advertising offer, affiliate token or redirect shared with unrelated controls is a supply-chain feature unless stronger evidence shows cluster-specific control. Record the control's result explicitly.

## Test alternatives

Ask:
- Does shared tenancy, a CDN, service provider, auction/reassignment or compromised victim explain the overlap?
- Is the supposedly rare artifact public, copied or generated by a common framework?
- Are domain control, content control and advertising account control actually the same relationship?
- Did the event occur within the attribution source's campaign interval?
- Are all corroborating publications copying the same upstream report?
- What observation would falsify the claim, and was that observation actually checked?

Distinguish evidence against a hypothesis from failure to find evidence. Retract a claim when its distinctive marker also occurs in a suitable unrelated control. A new collection gap must not restore a disproven claim.

Look for campaign-specific primary reporting from CISA, FBI, NSA, NCSC, CERT-UA, affected vendors and incident responders. Separate Russian state-associated activity from Russian-speaking criminal activity and from geographically hosted unrelated systems.

## Report confidence

Use low/moderate/high only with an explicit explanation of source independence, temporal fit, technical specificity, controls, contradictions and missing bridges. Use "unresolved" when evidence cannot distinguish the leading explanations. Reserve direct factual wording for directly supported facts; attribute official conclusions to their source.

Do not invent percentage confidence or imply that model agreement is corroboration. Keep severity and attribution confidence in separate fields.
