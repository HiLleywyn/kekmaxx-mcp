# Methodology

## Evidence ladder

1. Start with an attributable public record or named public organization.
2. Preserve the source URL, retrieval time, record identifier, and a content hash.
3. Separate what the source states from what the researcher infers.
4. Corroborate consequential claims with an independent source or a second
   official dataset.
5. Record negative findings as “not observed in this snapshot,” never as proof
   of absence.
6. Label uncertainty and plausible alternative explanations.

## Passive infrastructure comparison

DNS records, HTTP headers, certificates, analytics identifiers, CMS markers,
and shared hosting can establish technical proximity. They do not automatically
establish common ownership, editorial control, funding, or unlawful conduct.
Treat each edge according to what it actually proves.

## Political-ad and campaign-finance reconciliation

Google creative rows describe platform delivery ranges, targeting fields, and
advertiser identity. FEC independent-expenditure records describe reported
allocations. Join them by disclosed committee/advertiser identifiers and
geography while preserving these limitations:

- impression and spend fields may be ranges;
- candidate allocations may describe overlapping communications;
- reporting and delivery dates may differ; and
- platform archives and amended filings can change after collection.

Store the raw snapshot beside derived tables and make transformations
reproducible from scripts.

## Boundaries

This toolkit is for public-source research. Do not use it for credential use,
closed-group access, form submission, vulnerability exploitation, harassment,
targeted disruption, private-person identification, or publication of sensitive
personal data.
